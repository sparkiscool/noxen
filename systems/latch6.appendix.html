1. How it springs and stays latched

Two steel compression springs in parallel between the two tabs, under the pack. Each bolt's steel pin sits on a 20 deg ramp in the receiver, so a pull seats it harder; the springs only have to beat vibration and a crash's inertia on a slider. Not printed flexures (PCTG creeps under a standing preload) and not magnets (their force falls off as the bolt moves out, the wrong way round).

Two between the tabs wins on reliability: either spring failing leaves both bolts sprung at half force; with one per slider a lost spring leaves one bolt dead. Set 5 mm either side of the tab line they also keep each slider square in its channel. The rib's two notches sit near its points of zero bending: rib index 0.417 at the 43.5 N across (v5's one centre notch 0.607), 0.614 with the XT60 pull on top.

2. Holding the pack on the grabber, at any angle

v5 gripped pockets in the sliders. A slider floats 0.35 in its channel and bears 9 mm outboard of the grip, so a pull sagged the tab about 1.65 mm before anything was tight. v6 grips the cartridge: a wedge foot each side of the centre rib, a hook nub on each blade tip, four TPU-faced heel pads on the head under the cartridge's rim bands (x ±16.7, tab line ±21.5). The wedge is 10 deg: self-locking above a friction of 0.18, so the squeeze draws the cartridge onto the pads and a pull cannot open the blades. A 30 deg face would need friction above 0.58 or 21.7 N per blade to hold the 57 N XT60 pull.
