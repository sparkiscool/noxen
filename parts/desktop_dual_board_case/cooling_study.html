Dual-board case: cooling and airflow study

25 November 2026. Worked out on Fable 5.2 from the exact listing sizes, for the layout you asked for tonight: the whole cooling pad under the case, and the two sides opening over it.

The answer. Open the case to 120 mm between the boards' cooler faces (33 mm of travel on each side) and stand the pad 30 mm off the desk. That puts about 62 CFM of the pad's air between the boards, against 30 CFM with the case closed, and 13 CFM if the pad lies flat on the desk under the closed case. Each board's own cooler still does the real work; the pad's job is to hand it cool air from below and carry its hot air out of the top.

The layout to build

cool airhot air from the boards' own coolerspad fansboardscase walls

The path: in under the raised pad from all four sides, up through its five fans, up between the two boards into their cooler inlets, out of their fin outlets at the top, and the spare air out through the middle that opens when the sides slide apart.

  1. 1Pad level, with 30 mm clear under it all round: a printed dock of four corner risers. A pad lying flat on a desk cannot breathe. A filter sleeve round that gap is possible and costs about 11 % of the air.
  2. 2The case bridges the pad, it does not stand on the mesh. It lands on two saddles at the ends of the dock. Its bottom is an open frame: no floor plate, no base, no fan decks. Board bottom edges 25 mm above the mesh. Centre it on the big fan, which looks about 25 mm behind the pad's centre in the listing photo.
  3. 3Open to 120 mm: 33 mm on each side from the closed 54 mm. Give each side 53 mm of screw travel (a 160 mm gap) so the number can be tuned: try 100, 120 and 140 under load and keep the coolest. The rule behind it: each cooler face stands at the rim of the big fan, so the big fan blows straight up both cooler faces and the small fans sit under the boards' edges.
  4. 4Boards: cooler sides facing in, fin outlets up. Stand each board so the edge its fin stacks blow out of is the top edge. Outer walls solid, 13 mm outside each board: at 120 mm they stand outside all five fans, so no air is wasted.
  5. 5Lid: each half an open grille, no lid fan. The 66 mm slot that opens down the middle is the main way out. Leave 150 mm clear above.
  6. 6Spine: two end posts and the handle beam, 30 mm wide at most. Nothing standing in the middle of the gap; if a centre post is needed, one up to 40 mm wide straight over the big fan's hub, where the air is dead anyway. Fixed end plates 150 wide on the posts keep the ends shut when the sides are out.
  7. 7Motors, lead screws, the ESP32, driver and MOSFET boards go in the two end spaces beyond the boards, low down, never over the mesh. Lock both positions so the steppers hold with no current.
  8. 8Closed is for light work: about 170 W for both boards at a 10 °C air rise, because the four small fans are then mostly outside the box. Open carries about 350 W. Pad at full speed for heavy work.
  9. 9Projector, hubs, webcam hang outside and do not load the cooling. Keep the projector 60 mm or more from the nearest fin outlet.

What is wrong with the case as drawn today

The drawing in make.py still has the fans taken out of the pad and built into the base. As drawn it would move 5 to 22 CFM past the boards:

  1. 1The foot rails sit under the floor holes. The only way in from below is a tunnel 36 × 11 mm between the rails, open at its two far ends: 790 mm² for fans that sweep 20,300 mm².
  2. 2The fans have no proper seat. They are drawn standing on the floor deck, but their screw holes are in the deck above. Standing on the floor they pull through that tunnel only: about 5 CFM, and the three small fans push 3.7 L/s backwards through the weaker 120 mm fan.
  3. 3The base slots are too small either way. With the fans on the floor they are on the pressure side and leak. With the fans hung under the upper deck they are the intake, but at 4,180 mm² they are a fifth of the fan area: about 22 CFM.
  4. 4Round holes under flat boards. The board edges stand 8 mm over four round holes in the upper deck, so the two outer channels get almost nothing.
  5. 5The lid is one fifth open (10,150 mm²), and the fan under its middle pulls outside air in through the slots beside it instead of pulling air up from the boards.
  6. 6The four port windows (8,640 mm² together) let about a third of the air out low at one end, before it has passed the boards.
  7. 7The 120 mm fan sits partly under the end space, not under the boards.

What carries over to the new layout: nothing solid under a fan's mouth, 30 mm of clear height where the air comes in, no fan in the lid, the lid as open as its ribs allow, port windows cut to the real connectors.

What is not confirmed, and how much it matters

  1. Where the pad's small fans sit. It moves the best gap between 100 and 160 mm. One ruler measurement when the pad arrives settles it, and the screw travel above covers the whole range.
  2. How strong the Kootek fans are. It moves the air between 47 and 78 CFM open. The spacing and the layout are the same across that range.
  3. How much room the pad has under it lying flat. I assumed 5 mm. Whatever it is, the 30 mm dock makes it not matter.
  4. Where each board breathes in and blows out, and the board size. They decide which edge stands up and where the port windows go. The spacing does not depend on them.
  5. Heat per board. The watt figures above scale with the air rise you accept.
The earlier layout: fans taken out of the pad and built into the base

Worked out first, before you asked for the pad to stay whole under the case. Kept here because it is the other way to do it: a closed, filtered box that needs no opening sides. Reshaped (open floor on 30 mm feet, fans seated on the floor deck over a filter box, one long window under the boards, an open lid of 20,000 mm² or more, no lid fan) it moves about 55 CFM past the boards (42 to 68), and the air warms by 9 °C at 270 W against 22 °C as drawn. The lid fan adds 3 %, a fifth fan in the base 12 %. Fans off, the open box still draws about 6.2 L/s by itself, enough for roughly 112 W.

How it was worked out

Every opening is treated as a hole that costs pressure with the square of the air speed through it, every fan as a straight line from its free flow to the pressure where it stalls, and each space as one pressure; the flows are balanced. Where the pad's air goes at each gap is plain geometry: the share of each fan's circle that lies between the boards, under a board edge (split half and half) or outside. This is a first pass, good to about ±30 %: it ranks layouts and sizes openings, it does not predict a chip temperature. The model is tools/desktop_case_airflow.py in the repo and this page is built from it by tools/desktop_case_cooling_page.py. A second Fable 5.2 agent, given only the sizes and none of these conclusions, redid the closed-box sums with its own fan guesses and landed in the same place: 4 to 14 CFM as drawn, about 54 CFM reshaped, the intake as the choke, no fan in the lid. It prefers the fans hung under the upper deck over a filtered suction box, where this page seats them on the floor; both reach the same airflow. A third run scored the open spacing by the air rising within 30 mm of a cooler face and put the best at 110 to 120 mm; scored by all the air between the boards it keeps rising to 160. At 120 mm both scores are within 5 % of their best, so that is the setting.
