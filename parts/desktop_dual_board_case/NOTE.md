# Desktop dual-board case, upright suitcase (25 Nov)

Stands on its -x end the whole time it runs. Closed 372 x 126 x 299 mm plus 11 mm feet. No display screen (Spark plugs into other monitors), no fold-out legs, the front is a plain solid wall.

Carrying: the handle is on the +x short end wall with its grip turned to run along z (the case hangs from it at your side like a suitcase, big face against the leg); bar 100 long (70 clear), z 118..218, on two 3 mm doublers (the tile alone is margin 1.5, with the doubler 6). It hangs about 9 degrees off level because the projector bay sits below it (estimate; the grip centre is z 168, the centre of gravity about z 135).

Projector (S2-TEK / HOMPOW, 124.5 x 124.5 x 53.3 mm, 640 g, from the listing): in a bay on the same +x end wall, below the handle, lens looking out of that end. A 3 mm floor flush with the D1 deck reaches 160 mm out of the wall, with two 5 mm side lips; the projector lies flat, vents and ports on its side faces and the speaker grille on top all open; a 12 mm lip frame with an 80 x 36 lens window stands 1.5 mm in front of the lens so nothing touches it when the case is set down. The handle starts at z 118, 40 mm above the beam's edge, so it never shades it. The +x end is the one that stays up when you set the case down (it stands on the -x end). The lens position is an estimate: measure it on the bench and the window moves. The case could not hold the projector inside: the boards leave 15 mm at the end and the case is 126 deep, so the bay is outside the box.

Cooling layout, bottom up: foot rails with TPU pads | fan base (the Kootek pad's 120 mm and three 70 mm fans stand flat and blow UP) | bay with two boards standing upright side by side, hot sides facing a 60 mm chimney | lid that is a grille over its whole width plus the fifth 70 mm fan as an extractor over the chimney.

Air: in through the base's rear and -x end slots and the floor bores, up the chimney and over both boards, out the whole top and the upper end walls (above z 221, over the handle). The +x end wall of the base carries the projector bay and has no slots. Front and bay rear are solid. Exhaust about 25000 mm2 against about 16300 mm2 of floor bores plus the base slots.

Research (25 Nov, two searches: XPG, Tom's Hardware, Corsair, Digital Trends, plus the vendor white papers before): positive pressure (more intake than exhaust) keeps dust out of the gaps, so every intake gets a filter; filters add resistance, so a fan that pushes into them wants static pressure and the filter wants area; total clean airflow matters more than the pressure sign; fans low and pushing, exhaust high on a rising path, hot and cold apart. Nothing found on gap width between boards or laptop heatsink curves: the 60 mm gap is an estimate until the boards are measured. Sources: https://www.xpg.com/bl/knowledgehub/pc-chassis/108 , https://www.tomshardware.com/desktops/pc-building/how-to-optimize-your-pcs-airflow-using-positive-vs-negative-pressure , https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/pc-case-airflow-explained/ , https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/industrial-control-panel-cabinet-venting-best-practices , https://www.budind.com/article/basics-of-enclosure-cooling-white-paper/
Applied: 4 pushing intake fans against 1 extractor; the whole lid and the upper end walls as exhaust; the end slots moved above the handle; the front and bay rear solid so air goes along the boards; a fine nylon or foam filter sheet stuck on the inside of the base slots and over the floor bores (bought, cut to size; clean it monthly).

Box is fitted: width = the fan row (120 + three 70 + gaps + walls), depth = the 120 mm fan, height = board 220 + 8 standoff + 17 under the lid. Board sizes are estimates (outlines unpublished), so mounts wait for measuring.

48 flat PCTG tiles plus the handle, projector floor and lip frame, rails and TPU pads (about 2.0 kg), none over 180 mm, no supports.
