Probe bench: assembly guide

Build the probe's mast mechanism on the bench base, one step at a time. Each picture shows the new parts in colour, pulled back along the way they go in; grey parts are already on. Tick a step when it is done.

Read first

The turret needs a fix before the spool and motor go inChecked on the part models today: the spool cannot get into the printed turret (its small end sits inside a closed ring), and the motor's two M1.6 screws cannot be reached with a screwdriver. The Scout chat confirmed both and is fixing the turret now: either a cut you make in your print, or a reprint. Steps 1 to 6 do not depend on it: build those now. Steps 7 to 14 are the right order for when the fix arrives.
Two screw sizes changedThe spool axle is M3 × 50 with a nyloc nut (M3 × 40 is too short). The four turret bolts are M3 × 8 (M3 × 10 bottoms out in the inserts). Neither is in the M3 kit.

What you need

Printed (PCTG)

  • 1×bench base
  • 1×bench turret
  • 1×spool, with its 40-tooth gear
  • 2×pinion, 12 teeth (one is a spare)
  • 2×roller tube
  • 3roller saddles S1, S2, S3 (start with S2)
  • 1×head frame
  • 1×cradle (holds the camera)

Bought

  • 1×N20 gear motor, 6 V 100 rpm
  • 1×SG90 servo and its bag: the one-arm horn, its centre screw, the small horn screws, the 2 tab screws
  • 1×Arducam camera in its metal case, bracket off, with its own 2 side screws
  • 1×ZCZQC leaf battery contact (presses the roller)
  • 1×tape measure, 19 mm blade (gives blades A and B)
  • 6PTFE wires, 30 AWG, about 300 mm each
  • 1×3 mm carbon rod, cut to 23.8 mm
  • 1×kapton tape

Screws and inserts

  • 1×M3 × 50 and a nyloc nut (spool axle)
  • 4×M3 × 8 (turret to base)
  • 1×M3 × 10 (camera pivot)
  • 5×M3 heat-set insert, 5.7 long (4 base, 1 head frame)
  • 1×M2 × 4 (blade A)
  • 1×M2 × 6 and a nut (blade B)
  • 2×M1.6 × 4 (motor face: not usable on this turret, see step 8)

Tools and glue

  • soldering iron (inserts)
  • PH1 screwdriver, shaft 75 mm or longer
  • 2.2 mm drill or punch, a file, snips, gloves
  • 5-minute epoxy, CA glue, light oil
  • 2 clamps, a 5 V USB supply, a luggage scale

Build it

  1. Build now

    Nothing in steps 1 to 6 touches the turret.

    1. Heat-set one M3 insert into each of the base's four posts.
    2. Push each one straight down until it is flush with the top of its post.
    3. Let them cool.
    Uses: bench base · 4 inserts · soldering iron
    1. Wear gloves: cut tape is sharp.
    2. Cut two 200 mm strips from the tape measure. File the cut ends smooth. Call them A and B.
    3. Lay the 6 PTFE wires side by side on the curved-in face of A. Tape them down along its length with kapton. That is the ribbon.
    4. Scuff the paint off the last 10 mm of one end of each blade. That end goes into the spool.
    Uses: tape measure · 6 PTFE wires · kapton
    1. Cut the 3 mm carbon rod to 23.8 mm. Sand the ends square. Put one drop of light oil on it.
    2. Clip the leaf contact onto saddle S2: its flat back goes into the slit, the curved leaf sits on the thin tab's open face.
    3. Slide on a tube, then the saddle with its groove on the rod, then the other tube. The saddle sits in the middle.
    4. Keep S1 and S3 to hand: the saddle you fit sets how hard the leaf pushes.
    Uses: carbon rod · 2 roller tubes · saddle S2 · leaf contact · oil
    1. Heat-set one M3 insert into the hole in the small upright bracket, the one at the far end from the servo's ring. Let it cool.
    2. Push the one-arm horn onto the SG90 and fit its centre screw. Turn it by hand until the arm points straight up.
    3. Slide the SG90 into the closed ring from the open middle of the frame, until its two tabs stop on the ring's face. The horn faces the bracket.
    4. Drive the two tab screws through the tabs into the ring with the long PH1. Stop when snug: PCTG strips if you keep turning.
    Uses: head frame · 1 insert · SG90, horn, centre screw, 2 tab screws
    1. Slide the camera case into the cradle from the open back, lens first, until its front meets the lip. The lip is on the top and the sides only.
    2. Clamp it with its own two side screws through the slots in the side walls. The heads sit in the recesses.
    3. LED bars, if you have them: stick them on the case's top and bottom fronts through their windows. Not needed on the bench.
    Uses: cradle · camera and its 2 side screws
    1. Hold the cradle level, camera pointing forward. Lower it straight down between the bracket and the horn. The horn's arm, hub and screw slide up the channel in the cradle's wall.
    2. Put two of the small horn screws through the arm's holes into the slot above the pivot.
    3. Screw the M3 × 10 in from outside the bracket, through the insert, into the cradle's boss. Stop before it clamps: the cradle must nod freely on it.
    4. Nod the camera by hand from a little down to straight up. Nothing should rub.
    Uses: 2 small horn screws · M3 × 10

  8. When the fixed turret arrives

    These steps are the right order (checked on the models, confirmed by the Scout chat). Do not start them on the turret as printed.

    1. Stack the three scuffed ends: B, then the ribbon, then A. The two blades curve toward each other.
    2. Push all three together into the angled slot across the spool's hub, B on the hub side. Epoxy them in.
    3. Lead the ribbon's end through the hub and out of the small hub on the side away from the gear. Leave it loose.
    4. Let the epoxy set.
    Uses: spool · blades A and B with the ribbon · 5-minute epoxy
    1. Do this with the turret in your hand, before it goes on the base: the motor cannot go in afterwards.
    2. Press a pinion into its round pocket beside the gear-side wall, its small boss toward the wall. It is a snug fit.
    3. From behind the motor plate, push the N20's shaft through the hole in the motor plate and into the pinion, flat to flat, until the motor's face meets the plate. Tip the motor's tail down a hair to get it past the floor. The shaft's tip ends in the small hole in the wall.
    4. A drop of CA where the shaft meets the pinion.
    Motor screwsOn the turret as printed the two M1.6 screws cannot be driven and their heads would rub the pinion. The fixed turret will hold the motor another way; this step will say how.
    Uses: bench turret · pinion · N20 motor · CA
    1. From the spool's side, push the saddle into the middle pocket, leaf first. The leaf presses the pocket's far wall.
    2. Drop the rod's two ends into the two slots.
    Uses: the roller set from step 3
    1. Slide the spool in between the side walls from the open end, up and away from the throat. Gear on the motor plate's side, so it meets the pinion. The spool's coil holds the roller in.
    2. Push the M3 × 50 through the wall, the spool and the other wall, head on the gear side.
    3. Nyloc on the far side. Snug, not tight: the spool must spin freely.
    4. Turn the spool by hand: the gears should roll with no tight spot, and the two roller tubes should turn with it.
    This is the step that fails todayOn the turret as printed the spool's small end is trapped by a closed ring on the inside of the wall, so the spool cannot get in. Do not force it.
    Uses: spool with its blades · M3 × 50 · nyloc nut
    1. Lower the turret straight down onto the base's four posts. The motor hangs in the opening.
    2. Bolt it down with 4 × M3 × 8 from above, with the long PH1.
    3. Clamp the base to the table by its two end tabs.
    Uses: base with its inserts · 4 × M3 × 8 · 2 clamps
    1. Turn the spool by hand to wind the stack on, leaving about 160 mm free.
    2. Feed the free ends up through the throat: in at the flat slot at the bottom, out of the curved opening at the top.
    3. Run the mast out by hand, then push it back down. If the coil's outer turn lifts anywhere, swap saddle S2 for S3. If it holds and runs freely, try S1; keep the lightest saddle that holds.
    Uses: nothing new
    1. Slide the head frame's sleeve over the blade tips. Mark A through the top screw hole and B through the middle of the slot. Take it off.
    2. Drill or punch a 2.2 mm hole at each mark.
    3. Refit the sleeve: A on the M2 × 4 through its fixed hole; B on the M2 × 6 and nut through the slot, so B can slide.
    4. Leave the ribbon a 4 mm slack loop at the head, its end up through the slot in the plate.
    Uses: the built head · M2 × 4 · M2 × 6 and nut
    1. 5 V from a USB supply straight to the N20's two leads. Swap the leads to reverse.
    2. Stop by hand before either end of the travel: the motor has no end stop.
    3. Leave the SG90 unplugged and tilt the camera by hand.
    4. Keep fingers clear of the gears while the motor runs.
    Uses: 5 V USB supply

Then test

  1. Out and in, 20 times. Time one stroke (about 4 to 5 s for 150 mm). Watch for the coil lifting off the spool, the blades springing into a lens shape above the throat, and B's screw sliding in its slot.
  2. Gear mesh. Any tight spot, skip or click.
  3. Fold force. Mast fully out. Pull the head sideways with a luggage scale until the mast folds. Expect about 3.3 N (340 g).
  4. Tell Noxy what you saw. One line per test is enough; it goes to the Scout chat.
