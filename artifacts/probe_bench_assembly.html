What you need

Printed

  • 1×bench base
  • 1×bench turret
  • 1×spool (with the 40-tooth gear)
  • 2×pinion (one spare)
  • 2×roller tube (the roller file, printed twice)
  • 3roller saddles S1, S2, S3 (one each)
  • 1×head frame
  • 1×cradle (the sleeve)

Bought

  • 1×N20 motor, 6 V 100 rpm
  • 1×tape measure (19 mm blade)
  • 6PTFE wires, 30 AWG, ~300 mm
  • 1×ZCZQC leaf battery contact (presses the roller)
  • 1×SG90 servo, with the bag it came in: one arm horn, its centre screw, the small horn screws, the 2 tab screws
  • 1×Arducam camera in its metal case (bracket off) + its own 2 side screws

Hardware

  • 1×M3 × 40 + nyloc (spool axle)
  • 1×3 mm carbon rod, cut to 23.8 mm (roller axle)
  • 5×M3 × 10 (4 turret to base, 1 camera idler)
  • 5×M3 heat-set inserts, 5.7 long (4 bench base, 1 head frame)
  • 1×M2 × 4 (blade A)
  • 1×M2 × 6 + nut (blade B)
  • 2×M1.6 × 4 (motor face)
  • —5-min epoxy, CA, kapton, light oil
  • toolPH1 screwdriver, shaft 75 mm or longer

How it fits together

Assembly

    • Heat-set the 4 inserts into the bench base's four posts.
    • Bolt the bench turret on top with 4 × M3 × 10.
    • Clamp the base to the table by its end tabs.
    bench base · bench turret · 4 inserts · 4 × M3 × 10
    • Cut two 200 mm strips from the tape and file the cut edges smooth. Call them A and B.
    • Lay the 6 PTFE wires side by side on A's curved-in face and tape them along its length with kapton. That is the ribbon.
    • Scuff the paint off the last 10 mm of one end of each blade, the end that goes into the spool.
    tape · 6 PTFE wires · kapton
    • Stack the three spool ends: B, then the ribbon, then A, the two blades' curves facing each other.
    • Push all three ends together into the angled slot across the spool's hub, B on the hub side, and epoxy them in.
    • Run the ribbon's end through the hub and out of the small hub on the side opposite the gear. Leave it loose.
    spool · 5-min epoxy
    • Cut the 3 mm carbon rod to 23.8 mm, sand the ends square, and put a drop of light oil on it.
    • Clip the battery contact onto the S2 saddle's thin tab, from the slit end: the contact's flat back goes into the slit, the curved leaf sits on the tab's open face.
    • Slide a tube, the saddle (groove down) and the second tube onto the rod, saddle in the middle.
    • From the spool's side, push the saddle into the turret's middle pocket, leaf first, and drop the rod's ends into the two slots. The leaf presses on the pocket's far wall.
    • Set the spool between the turret's side walls, gear on the side with the motor plate. Its coil holds the roller in place.
    • Push an M3 × 40 through the walls and the spool. Nyloc on the far side, snug, not tight: the spool must spin freely.
    2 roller tubes · saddle S2 · 1 contact · carbon rod 23.8 mm · spool · M3 × 40 + nyloc
    • Press a pinion onto the N20 shaft, flat to flat, all the way on. A drop of CA.
    • Screw the motor's face to the turret's motor plate with 2 × M1.6 × 4, so the pinion meshes with the spool's gear.
    • Turn the spool by hand: the gears should roll with no tight spot.
    N20 · pinion · 2 × M1.6 × 4 · CA
    • Turn the spool by hand: the two roller tubes should turn with the coil and the rod should sit down in its slots.
    • Nothing to adjust: the saddle you fit (S1, S2 or S3) sets how hard the leaf pushes. Start with S2.
    —
    • Turn the spool by hand to wind the stack on, leaving about 160 mm free.
    • Feed the free ends up through the throat: in at the flat slot at the bottom, out of the curved opening at the top.
    • Test the roller: run the mast fully out, then push it back down by hand. If the coil's outer turn lifts anywhere, swap S2 for S3. If it holds and the mast runs freely, try S1 too; keep the lightest saddle that holds.
    —
    • Slide the head frame's sleeve over the blade tips. Mark A through the top screw hole and B through the middle of the slot. Take it off.
    • Drill or punch a 2.2 mm hole at each mark.
    • Refit the sleeve: A on the M2 × 4 through its fixed hole, B on the M2 × 6 and nut through the slot, so B can slide.
    • Leave the ribbon a 4 mm slack loop at the head, its end up through the slot in the plate.
    • Heat-set one insert into the hole in the bracket on the frame's idler side (the small upright plate opposite the servo's ring).
    head frame · M2 × 4 · M2 × 6 + nut · 1 insert
    • Push the arm horn onto the SG90's spline with its centre screw, then turn the output by hand until the arm points straight up.
    • Slide the SG90 into the frame's closed ring from the open side, where the cradle will go, until its tabs stop on the ring's face. The horn ends up facing the bracket.
    • Drive its two tab screws through the tabs into the ring's pilot holes with the long PH1, reaching past the bracket. Stop when snug; PCTG strips if you keep turning.
    head frame · SG90 + horn · centre screw · 2 tab screws
    • Slide the camera case into the cradle from the open back, lens first, until its front meets the lip. The lip runs along the top and the sides only.
    • Clamp it with its own two side screws through the slots in the side walls. The heads sit in the recesses.
    • If you have the LED bars, stick them on the case's top and bottom fronts through their windows. Skip them on the bench if you don't.
    cradle · camera + 2 side screws
    • Hold the cradle level (camera pointing forward) and lower it straight down between the bracket and the horn. The horn's hub, centre screw and arm drop into the channel on the cradle's wall from its open bottom edge.
    • Put two of the small horn screws through the arm's holes into the slot above the axis.
    • Screw an M3 × 10 in from outside the bracket, through its insert, into the cradle's boss. Stop before it clamps: the cradle must nod freely on it.
    • Nod the camera by hand from 20° down to straight up. Nothing should rub.
    2 horn screws · M3 × 10
    • 5 V from a USB supply straight to the N20's two leads. Swap the leads to reverse.
    • Stop by hand before either end of the travel: the motor has no end stop.
    • The driver board is not needed on the bench. Leave the SG90 unplugged and tilt the camera by hand.
    5 V USB supply · 2 leads
Careful: cut tape edges are sharp. Wear gloves for steps 2 and 8, and keep fingers clear of the gears when the motor runs.

Tests and results

Time each stroke (expect about 4–5 s for 150 mm). Watch for the coil lifting off the spool, the blades springing into the lens above the throat, and B's screw sliding in its slot.
